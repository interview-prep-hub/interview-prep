# Tell Me About Yourself

> **Tier:** T1 (know cold) · **Related:** [STAR stories bank](02-star-stories-bank.md), [Common HR questions](03-common-hr-questions.md), [Questions to ask](05-questions-to-ask.md), [Resume line-by-line defense](06-resume-line-by-line-defense.md), [Interview formats](../00-start-here/03-interview-formats.md), [Final-year project](../16-ai-and-final-year-project/02-human-activity-recognition-project.md)
> **On your resume:** all of it. This answer is the trailer for your resume, and the interviewer picks follow-up questions from it.

## Quick revision
- **Formula:** Present (who you are and what you do now) -> Past (how you got here, with two or three proof points) -> Future (what you want next and why this role).
- **Length:** 60 to 90 seconds by default. Keep a 30-second version for quick screens and a 2-minute version for when they say "take your time".
- **Rule of defensibility:** say only what you can discuss for 20 minutes. Every noun you mention is a possible follow-up.
- **Tailor the first two sentences** to the job: Rails, JS/TS, React Native or Django.
- **Scale numbers with attribution:** "a platform serving 700+ game stores", never "I served 700+ stores".
- **End with a hook and a reason:** a line about what you want next and why this company, so the conversation moves forward.
- **Avoid:** reciting the resume line by line, starting from school, listing every technology, personal life details nobody asked for, criticizing past employers, filler like "I'm a hard worker".
- **"Walk me through your resume"** is the chronological cousin: 2 to 3 minutes, oldest to newest, explaining each transition.

## 1. Why this question matters
It is usually the first question, so it sets the tone and the agenda. A clear answer tells the interviewer three things at once: whether you communicate well, whether your experience matches the role, and what to ask next. That last part is your advantage: whatever you put in the answer becomes the menu for the rest of the interview, so put your strongest, best-prepared material there.

What interviewers listen for:
- **Relevance:** does the experience match the role?
- **Clarity:** a structure they can follow, at a calm pace.
- **Ownership:** what you did vs what the team did.
- **Honesty:** numbers attributed correctly, no inflated titles.
- **Motivation:** why you are talking to them in particular.

## 2. The present-past-future framework

| Part | What goes in | Time | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | Current title, company, stack, the kind of work you do, one or two headline projects | About 40% | A list of every task you have ever done |
| Past | Earlier roles and education that explain your path, with one proof point each | About 35% | Childhood, school marks, every course |
| Future | What you want next, why this role and company fit | About 25% | "I want to grow" with nothing specific |

Why present first: it is your strongest material and what the interviewer cares about most. The past then explains how you got there, and the future connects you to their job.

What to keep as the time shrinks:

| Time you have | Keep | Cut |
|---|---|---|
| One sentence | Current role, stack, one headline project | Everything else |
| 30 seconds | Current role, CrystalCommerce and the Coloc app in one line each, your next step | Internships, TA role, education |
| 60 to 90 seconds | The above, plus internships, TA role and education in two sentences | Project internals |
| 2 minutes | The above, plus one hard problem and the FYP | Nothing important; just do not ramble |

## 5. The 90-second version (your default)

> "I'm a full-stack software engineer based in Lahore, with over a year of professional experience. I'm currently an Associate Software Engineer at Devntech, where I build full-stack features for international clients in Rails, React, TypeScript and React Native with Expo.
>
> Two client projects make up most of that work. On CrystalCommerce, a legacy Rails e-commerce platform serving 700+ game stores, I integrated PriceCharting as a sales channel, with background jobs that sync listings, prices, stock and orders, and I imported PriceCharting's market prices into the catalog so stores can price their inventory from market data. On Colochousing, a Belgian co-living platform, I work on the Rails 7 web app and REST API, I've built features in the Coloc resident app, which is live on the App Store and Google Play, and I contributed to migrating onto a new multi-tenant TypeScript platform.
>
> Before Devntech, I did a month of Rails training at Visnext and two internships in 2025: at PureLogics I proposed and built a Coursera-style LMS with Django REST Framework and React, and at RemoteDev I built backend APIs with Next.js and MongoDB. I studied IT at PUCIT, where I was also a teaching assistant for programming, data structures, databases and web development labs.
>
> Now I'm looking for [your concrete next step], and [one specific thing about this company] is why I applied here."

Notes on this version:
- Roughly 200 words, which is about 90 seconds at a calm pace.
- "Over a year" counts from the July 2025 internships, so it holds from mid-2026 onward; keep it accurate as time passes and be ready to break it down ("about a year at Devntech, plus internships before that").
- Two projects get detail; everything else gets one line. That focus is deliberate.

> **Fill in:** "two client projects make up most of that work": confirm this is true for your time at Devntech, or name the other work too.

Why each part is there, and what it invites:

| Part | Purpose | Follow-up it invites |
|---|---|---|
| Role, stack, international clients | Instant relevance and level | "What does a typical week look like?" |
| CrystalCommerce sentence | Backend depth: integrations and background jobs at platform scale | "Explain the PriceCharting integration" |
| Colochousing sentence | Range: web, API, mobile, migration | "Which app features did you build?" |
| Visnext and internships | A path with initiative (proposed the LMS) and range (Python, TypeScript) | "Why did you leave Visnext after a month?" |
| PUCIT and the TA role | Fundamentals and communication | "What did you teach?" |
| Future | Motivation and fit | "Why this company?" |

Future-part examples to adapt (keep only what is true for you):
- Rails product company: "a team with a mature Rails product, where I can own features end to end and go deeper on performance and architecture."
- JS/TS startup: "a TypeScript-first team where I can work across the API and the frontend and help shape the product."
- React Native role: "a mobile-focused team where I can own app features from the API to the store release."
- Remote international team: "a remote team where clear written communication and ownership matter, which is how I already work with clients."

## 10. Weak vs strong: a worked rewrite
A weak answer built from the same resume facts:

> "I graduated in BS IT from PUCIT in 2025. I have done internships at PureLogics and RemoteDev and I worked at Visnext and now Devntech. My skills are Ruby, TypeScript, JavaScript, Python, C#, Java, C, C++, SQL, Rails, Node.js, Django, ASP.NET Core, GraphQL, React, React Native, Next.js, PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, Redis, Firebase and Docker. I am hard-working, a quick learner and a team player. I am looking for a good opportunity where I can learn and grow."

What is wrong with it:

| Problem | Effect |
|---|---|
| Starts in the past | The interviewer waits 20 seconds to learn what you do now |
| Company names without what you did | Sounds like attendance, not achievement |
| 24 technologies | Each one is now a possible test, and none of them sticks |
| Self-praise adjectives | Unverifiable; every candidate says them |
| "A good opportunity to learn and grow" | Says nothing about this company or what you offer |

The strong version (section 5) fixes each point: current role first, two projects with concrete outcomes from the resume, the earlier path in two sentences, and a specific next step. The adjectives disappear because the facts show them: proposing the LMS shows initiative, shipping to both app stores shows delivery, two years as a TA shows communication.

## 12. Close cousins of this question

**"Describe yourself in three words."** Pick words you can prove with resume facts, and give the proof in the same breath. Examples of words and the evidence that supports them:
- *Adaptable:* Python (Django), TypeScript (Next.js, React Native, oRPC) and Ruby (Rails) across four roles since July 2025.
- *Proactive:* you proposed the LMS project at PureLogics instead of waiting for an assignment.
- *Clear explainer:* two years helping students in PUCIT labs as a teaching assistant.

> **Fill in:** your three words, each with one fact.

**"Tell me something that is not on your resume."** A true, small, human detail that connects to work: how you learn, a side interest, something you built for fun, a value you hold.
> **Fill in:** your answer.

**"What are you most proud of?"** A STAR story, not a list. Use the "proudest achievement" entry in [the story bank](02-star-stories-bank.md).

**"Introduce yourself" in a panel interview.** Greet the panel, use the 60-second version, and name the role you are interviewing for so every panelist has the same context.

**"Why did you choose software (or IT)?"** One honest sentence plus what keeps you in it now.
> **Fill in:** your answer.

## 13. When a foreign client interviews you
Software houses often let a client interview a developer before assigning them to the client's project. The intro changes slightly:
- Lead with the stack that matches their project and one comparable project.
- Mention that you already work with international clients at Devntech, and how you communicate (written updates, calls, overlap hours).
- Keep it to about 60 seconds; clients often have a fixed slot.
- Never share other clients' confidential details; describe projects at the level of your resume.
- End with a question about their project, which shows interest and turns the call into a conversation.

> **Fill in:** the overlap hours you can commit to for European and North American clients, so you can answer instantly.

## 14. The written version
For recruiter messages, job portals and cover notes, compress the same story.

- **One-line headline:** "Full-stack engineer: Ruby on Rails, React, TypeScript, React Native (Expo). Associate Software Engineer at Devntech."
- **Three-sentence message:** "I'm a Lahore-based full-stack engineer at Devntech, building features for international clients in Rails, React, TypeScript and React Native. ## 15. Tricky follow-ups about your profile
These often come right after the intro. Answer calmly with facts; never argue with the question.

**"You have about a year of experience; this role asks for two or more."**
Do not dispute the requirement; show what your year contains: production work on two client platforms (a legacy Rails monolith and a Rails, React Native and TypeScript platform in migration), an app live on both stores, a third-party integration built on background jobs, plus two internships. Then ask what the extra experience represents for them (owning features end to end, mentoring, on-call) and answer that directly.

**"Rails, Django, Next.js, React Native: what are you, really?"**
"Full-stack, with Rails as my deepest backend and React and React Native on the front end. The internships gave me Python and Next.js, which is why picking up the TypeScript platform at Devntech felt familiar." Then tilt toward the role's stack. Adjust the last part if it does not match your experience.

**"Several positions in a short time: why?"**
Put them in context: the TA role was part-time during your degree, both internships were fixed-term summer roles, Visnext was a one-month period (give your honest reason, see [common HR questions](03-common-hr-questions.md)), and Devntech is your first long-term role.

**"Are you a fresher?"**
"I graduated in 2025. I've been working professionally since then, at Devntech since November 2025, after two internships and a month of Rails training." Let the projects carry the weight.

**"Have you led anything?"**
Do not invent a lead role. Point to real signals: proposing and building the LMS, owning features you shipped, explaining concepts to students as a TA.
> **Fill in:** any feature or area you owned end to end, or any time you helped a newer teammate.

## 16. Delivery tips
- **Practice out loud, not in your head.** Record yourself on your phone, listen once, cut 20%.
- **Memorize the skeleton, not the script.** Keep three or four anchor phrases (Devntech and stack, CrystalCommerce, Colochousing, internships, next step) and speak naturally between them.
- **Pace:** slower than feels natural; pause between present, past and future.
- **Numbers land when they are short:** "700+ game stores", "live on both app stores for 400+ residents".
- **Remote interviews:** look at the camera when you make your key points, use headphones, and do not read from the screen; interviewers notice eye movement.
- **Language:** most technical interviews in Pakistan are in English; if an interviewer switches to Urdu, it is fine to follow, keeping technical terms in English.
- **Close cleanly:** end on the future part and stop. Silence after a good answer is fine; the interviewer will pick it up.
- **If they interrupt with a question,** answer it briefly, then decide whether to finish the intro; usually they have heard enough, so follow their lead.
- **Energy without performance:** sit upright, smile at the start, and let your voice rise slightly on the project names; enthusiasm reads as interest, not arrogance.
- **Nerves:** the first 15 seconds are the hardest, so make your first sentence the one you have said most often. A slow breath before you start costs nothing.
- **Do not apologize for your experience level** ("I only have one year, but..."). State facts and let them judge.

## 17. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Starting with school or childhood | Wastes the strongest 20 seconds | Start with your current role |
| Reading the resume line by line | They have it already; it is boring | Tell the story; pick two proof points |
| Listing 20 technologies | Nothing sticks, and each one invites a test | Name the stack of your main projects; let the skills section do the rest |
| "I built a system for 8M customers" | Misattributed scale; easy to expose | "A platform serving 700+ stores and 8M+ customers; my part was ..." |
| "We did X" for everything | Hides your contribution | "The team did X; I built Y" |
| Badmouthing a past employer | Raises red flags about you | Neutral, forward-looking language |
| No ending | The interviewer has to rescue you | End with what you want next and why them |
| Over three minutes | Attention drops | 60 to 90 seconds unless asked for more |
| Inventing a passion story | Falls apart on the first follow-up | Use a true, small, specific reason |
| Inconsistent dates | Looks careless or dishonest | Rehearse the timeline in section 8 |
