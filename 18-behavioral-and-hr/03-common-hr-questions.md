## Quick revision
- **Formula for most HR answers:** an honest fact, framed positively, linked forward to this role. Two or three sentences, then stop.
- **Never lie and never badmouth.** Dates, titles, CGPA and salary can be checked; criticism of past employers makes you the risk.
- **Know your timeline cold:** TA during your degree, two parallel summer internships, one month at Visnext, Devntech since November 2025.
- **Why looking:** what you gained at Devntech plus a specific next step this role offers. Not "money" alone, not "my manager".
- **Why hire you:** match (their needs, your experience), proof (two resume facts), value (how you work).
- **Weakness:** real, not fatal for the role, with concrete steps and evidence of progress. No "perfectionist".
- **Visnext after a month:** one or two true, neutral sentences, what you gained (Rails MVC, ActiveRecord, Hotwire), then your stability since.
- **Two internships:** planning, transparency, delivery on both; never exaggerate hours.
- **Time zones:** Pakistan is UTC+5 all year; Belgium is 3 to 4 hours behind, the UK 4 to 5, US Eastern 9 to 10.
- **AI coding tools:** follow company policy, keep client code and secrets out of unapproved tools, understand and test every line you commit.
- Salary questions live in [salary and offers](04-salary-and-offers.md); your questions for them in [questions to ask](05-questions-to-ask.md).

## 1. What HR rounds look for
HR and hiring-manager rounds check what technical rounds do not: communication, motivation, stability, culture fit, honesty and logistics (notice period, salary, location, shift timings). At small companies the CEO or a team lead may run this round. Most "bad" HR outcomes come from three things: inconsistent stories, negativity about past employers, and vague motivation. Each section below gives a framework, a sample built only from your resume facts, and the details you must fill in.

## 2. "Why are you looking for a change?"
You joined Devntech in November 2025, so expect this question, plus "why so soon?" if you are interviewing within a year or so.

**Framework:** (1) what you gained where you are, (2) what you want next, specifically, (3) why this role offers it.

**Honest reasons that work (use only true ones):**
- You want to go deeper in one area (Rails product work, React Native, TypeScript platforms) instead of spreading across client projects.
- You want product ownership (a product company) after services work, or broader client exposure after a single product.
- You want stronger mentorship or a team with senior engineers in your stack.
- You want a remote or international team.
- You want compensation aligned with your current skills; say it calmly, as one factor among others.
- Relocation, commute or schedule.

**Sample (adapt):**
> "Devntech has given me a lot in a short time: a legacy Rails e-commerce platform, a Rails 7 app with a React Native app on top, and a migration to a TypeScript platform, all for international clients. Now I want [your specific next step, for example to own features in one product end to end], and this role offers that because [specific fact about the company]."

**"Why so soon?"** (use this shape only if it is true for you)
> "I'm not leaving because something is wrong; I'm moving toward [specific next step], and this role is a rare match for it. I want my next move to be a long one."

> **Fill in:** your real reason in one sentence, and the specific thing this company offers that your current role does not.

Answers to avoid, and what they signal:

| Avoid | Signals | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "My manager is bad" / "The company is toxic" | You might say the same about them | What you want next |
| "Only for salary" | You will leave for the next small raise | Salary as one factor, after growth and fit |
| "There is nothing to learn there" | Arrogance, or a lack of curiosity | What you learned, and what you want to learn next |
| "I'm bored" | Low ownership | The kind of challenge you are looking for |
| A long story of grievances | Drama | Two sentences and a pivot to the future |

## 3. "Why this company?"
**Research before every interview:** the product and its users, the stack (job post, engineering blog, public repositories), recent news, how they work (remote, team size), and values they state publicly.

**Framework:** two specific facts about them, each linked to your experience.

> "You're building [product] on [stack], and that's close to what I do every day: [matching experience from your resume]. I'm also drawn to [second fact: domain, team setup, growth path], because [true personal reason]."

**Avoid:** "good environment", "good learning", "reputed company". Every candidate says them.

> **Fill in:** before each interview, two facts about the company and the matching piece of your experience.

## 4. "Why should we hire you?"
**Framework:** match, proof, value, in under a minute.

**Sample for a Rails and React full-stack role (adapt the emphasis to the job):**
> "Three reasons. First, the match: you need someone who works across Rails and React, and that's what I do at Devntech for international clients, in a legacy Rails monolith and in a Rails 7 app with React on Rails and Sidekiq. Second, proof that I deliver: I integrated PriceCharting as a sales channel for a platform serving 700+ game stores, with background jobs syncing listings, prices, stock and orders, and I've built features in a React Native app that's live on both app stores. Third, how I work: I went from a month of Rails training to production Rails work, I proposed the project I built during my PureLogics internship, and two years as a lab TA made me comfortable explaining technical ideas."

**Variations:**
- **React Native role:** lead with the Coloc app (events, tasks, split bills, rent payments, push notifications, EAS releases).
- **JS/TS role:** lead with the TypeScript migration (oRPC, TanStack Start, Drizzle, org-scoped access control) and RemoteDev's Next.js work.
- **Django role:** lead with the LMS (DRF, subscriptions, role-based dashboards), then transferable backend depth from Rails.

> **Fill in:** one closing line tied to this company's specific need from the job description.

## 6. "What is your greatest weakness?"
**Framework:** a real weakness, its impact, what you are doing about it, and evidence that it is improving. It must not be a core requirement of the job (do not say "I struggle with JavaScript" for a React role).

**Answers that fail:** "I'm a perfectionist", "I work too hard", "I have no weaknesses", or a weakness with no plan.

**Common honest weaknesses at your level (use one only if it is true for you):**

| Weakness | Improvement you could describe |
|---|---|
| Optimistic estimates | Breaking tasks down, adding time for tests and review, flagging risks early |
| Waiting too long before asking for help | A personal time box (for example, ask after a set time stuck), with notes on what you tried |
| Limited system design experience at large scale | Studying design case studies and applying them to your own projects |
| Spreading across many stacks | Choosing one stack to go deep in and building depth on purpose |
| Public speaking or presenting to clients | Volunteering for demos and preparing notes in advance |
| Testing discipline | Writing tests first for bug fixes, adding tests in every pull request |

**Sample shape:**
> "Early on I gave optimistic estimates, because I underestimated testing and integration time. Now I break work into smaller tasks, add explicit time for tests and review, and tell my lead about risks the day I see them. [One piece of evidence that it is working.]"

> **Fill in:** your real weakness, what you have done about it, and one piece of evidence.

## 7. "Where do you see yourself in five years?"
**Framework:** a direction, not a title; skills you will build; contribution to the team; consistent with the company's growth path.

> "In five years I want to be a strong senior full-stack engineer: someone who can design a feature end to end, from the data model and API to the web or mobile UI, review others' work well and help newer developers grow. Over the next year or two that means going deeper in [your chosen area], which is a big reason this role appeals to me."

Avoid "in your seat", vague "manager" ambitions without substance, and, for a local company, announcing plans to move abroad soon; it signals a short stay. If you do have such plans, do not lie; simply focus on what you want to achieve in this role.

> **Fill in:** your real direction (backend, mobile, full-stack, product, leadership) and the next concrete skill.

## 8. "Why did you leave Visnext after a month?"
Your resume shows Visnext Solutions, Associate Software Engineer, October 2025, with one month of Ruby on Rails training (MVC, ActiveRecord, Hotwire), followed by Devntech in November 2025. Interviewers ask to check stability and honesty, not to trap you.

**Rules:** one or two true sentences of reason, no blame, then what you gained, then evidence of stability. Do not over-explain; long justifications sound defensive.

**Framing options for common real reasons (pick the one that is true):**

| Real reason | Neutral framing |
|---|---|
| A better-fitting offer arrived | "During that month I received an offer from Devntech for client work in Rails, React and React Native, the kind of production work I wanted, so I took it." |
| The role was different from what you expected | "The role turned out to be mostly training, and I wanted to work on real client projects sooner. When Devntech offered that, I moved." |
| The training period ended without a placement (the company's decision) | "The month was a training period that didn't continue into a role. I took the Rails foundation from it and joined Devntech the following month." |
| Terms, commute or schedule did not work | "The terms didn't work for me long term, and I preferred to move early rather than stay in a role I knew I would leave." |
| The company changed plans | "Plans on their side changed after I joined, so I moved on quickly." |

Then close the loop:
> "The training was useful: it gave me the Rails foundation (MVC, ActiveRecord, Hotwire) for the client work I've done at Devntech since November 2025."

**If they ask "Will you leave us after a month too?"**
> "No. Visnext was a one-month training period early in my career; I've been at Devntech since November 2025, and I'm looking for a place to grow for the long term."

> **Fill in:** the real reason, phrased as one neutral sentence, and whether you left on good terms (mention it only if true).

What not to say about Visnext:
- "They were unprofessional" or any other judgment of the company.
- A long account of what went wrong; one sentence is enough.
- Anything that contradicts your resume dates or title.
- "I was just there for the training" (it suggests you take roles without intending to stay); describe the facts instead.

## 9. "How did you manage two internships at the same time?"
From July to September 2025 you were a Python (Django) Full-Stack Intern at PureLogics (onsite) and a Full-Stack Intern at RemoteDev (remote). Interviewers want to know three things: were you honest with both, did you deliver, and would you take a second job now?

**Framework:** structure (how the hours fit), transparency (who knew what), delivery (what each got), and your present focus.

> "From July to September 2025 I did two internships: PureLogics onsite during office hours, and RemoteDev remotely [when: for example evenings, with agreed check-in times]. It worked because I planned each week in advance, kept both teams informed about my availability, and treated deadlines seriously. At PureLogics I proposed and built an LMS with DRF and React; at RemoteDev I built and integrated backend APIs with Next.js and MongoDB. Now I'm focused on one full-time role."

- Never claim more hours than you worked.
- If either team did not know about the other, do not volunteer a false "both knew"; keep the answer factual about how you split the time.
- If asked about side work now, answer according to your current contract and the policy of the company you are applying to.

> **Fill in:** how the hours actually fit, whether both teams knew, one clash you handled, and your current stance on side projects or freelance work.

What interviewers listen for in this answer: whether the work at one place suffered, whether you were open about it, and whether you plan to repeat it while employed with them. A calm, specific answer that ends with "now I am focused on one full-time role" (if true) closes all three.

## 10. "What was your CGPA?"
State it exactly, without rounding up; it can be checked against your transcript. Some companies use a CGPA cutoff for recent graduates. If yours is below a cutoff, say it plainly, then point to practical evidence: production work at Devntech, the internships and two years as a TA.

> **Fill in:** your CGPA and, if it needs context, one honest sentence (for example courses you excelled in, or time spent on the TA role and projects).
The same rule applies to your FYP grade or any course grade they ask about: exact numbers, no rounding, then evidence of what you can do now.

## 16. "How do you learn a new stack quickly?"
**Method:** official guides and docs first; a tiny project to get hands-on; reading the existing codebase to learn its conventions; mapping new concepts to ones you know (Django models to ActiveRecord, Rails jobs to Sidekiq workers, Express routes to oRPC procedures); asking for an early code review.

**Evidence from your resume:** a month of Rails training (MVC, ActiveRecord, Hotwire) followed by Rails client work at Devntech; contributing to a TypeScript platform built with oRPC, TanStack Start and Drizzle; building React Native features with Expo.

> **Fill in:** which of these you learned on the job, how long it took to become productive, and the resource that helped most.

## 17. "Do you use AI coding tools? How?"
Many companies now ask this. They want to know whether you use tools responsibly and whether you can still think for yourself. Keep the answer generic; there is no need to name products.

**What a good answer covers:**
- **Policy first:** you follow the employer's and the client's rules on which tools are allowed.
- **Confidentiality:** you never paste client code, credentials or personal data into a tool the company has not approved; NDAs apply to prompts too.
- **Good uses:** boilerplate, exploring an unfamiliar API (then checking the official docs), test ideas, explaining unfamiliar code, refactoring suggestions.
- **Verification:** you read and understand every line before committing it, run the tests, and check edge cases, security (injection, authorization) and licensing.
- **Ownership:** you are accountable for code you commit, so you never submit code you cannot explain in review.
- **Skills:** you still practice solving problems without tools, which matters in interviews and when tools are wrong.

> "I use coding assistants for boilerplate, for exploring unfamiliar APIs and for test ideas, always within the tools my company allows and never with client code in unapproved tools. I treat a suggestion like a pull request from a stranger: I read it, test it and check it against the docs before it goes anywhere near a commit, because I'm the one accountable for it."

> **Fill in:** what your current company's policy is, and how you actually use such tools day to day (or that you mostly don't).

**"What would your manager or lead say about you?"** Quote real feedback you have received (a review, a comment on a pull request, a message from a lead), including one area they asked you to improve.

Pick the true ones.

**"What does your current role lack?"** Frame it as what you want more of, not what is wrong: "more ownership of X", "deeper work in Y".

## 26. Never do this
- Lie about dates, titles, CGPA, salary or offers.
- Present a platform's scale (700+ stores, 8M+ customers, 16K+ users) as your personal achievement.
- Criticize a past employer, client, manager or teammate.
- Share confidential client details, code or internal numbers.
- Say "I have no weaknesses" or give a disguised strength as a weakness.
- Say you are only there for the money, or ask about leave and holidays before anything else.
- Promise to skip your notice period, or accept an offer you intend to break.
- Read prepared answers off the screen in a video interview.
- Argue with the interviewer or talk over them.
- End without a question of your own.
